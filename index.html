01 · Diagnostic

Where the funnel was leaking

A full-funnel audit (heatmaps, session recordings, funnel analytics) revealed the issue was not at the top of the funnel. Meta campaigns were working. Visitors were arriving with intent. The drop happened on the product page, before the decision to buy was ever made.

Stage 01
Homepage → PDP
Strong hero narrative, benefit icons, media strip (CBS, ABC, MSN, Yahoo)
Low friction
Stage 02
PDP → Add to Cart
Information overload, size guide hidden, no pain-to-solution narrative, weak urgency
High friction ↑
Stage 03
Cart → Checkout

Trust signals built on PDP vanish at the highest anxiety moment of the journey

The PDP was doing too much and not enough simultaneously. It presented rich visual content (9 images, GIFs, infographics) but failed to sequence information in the order a pain-motivated buyer actually needs it.

02 · Persona

Two buyers. Two different levers.

Understanding who arrives from Meta and what they're actually trying to solve changes everything about the page's job.

Margaret, 52
Primary Buyer · 70% of traffic

Active lifestyle, chronic foot or joint pain. She researches before buying, reads reviews specifically for pain-related keywords, and is skeptical of DTC brands. She doesn't convert on features; she converts on trust built progressively through the page. One unanswered question sends her back to Google.

David, 47
Secondary Buyer · High AOV potential

Purchasing for a partner or parent. More decisive, bundle-receptive, and converts faster when size guidance is unambiguous and the gift rationale is spelled out. Responds to "proven medical solution" framing over product specs. Price is less of an objection than confidence in the right choice.

03 · Strategic Levers

Five levers. One priority order.

01

PDP Narrative Restructuring

The benefit icons (Wide Toe Box, Natural Alignment, Zero Drop) appeared as isolated claims with no bridge to the customer's specific condition. Resequence into a pain-to-solution narrative: open with the problem, explain the mechanism, close with proof. The "Healthy Barefoot Shoes" headline works for SEO, not for a visitor with plantar fasciitis looking for relief.

Target: +15–25% Add-to-Cart
02

Inline Size Guide

Footwear has one of the highest return rates in e-commerce. The size chart required a click to reveal: one extra step for a customer already uncertain about buying shoes from a DTC brand online. Embed a simplified inline guide directly under the size selector. Add a review-sourced recommendation line: "Fits true to size / Size up if wide feet."

Target: +8–12% CVR
03

Bundle Architecture Reframe

The "2 pairs: 10% off" bundle had no convincing rationale. Nobody needs two identical shoes. Reposition as a cross-SKU use-case bundle: "One for everyday. One for active days." Replace the countdown timer (urgency fatigue for a considered health purchase) with value-stacking: "2 pairs = 12 months of pain-free walking under $130."

Target: +18–22% multi-item rate
04

Trust Continuity at Checkout

The "Developed with podiatrists" claim, the brand's strongest credibility signal, disappeared entirely at checkout. That's the exact moment purchase anxiety peaks. Inject a persistent reassurance strip into the order summary column: podiatrist endorsement, return policy, shipping, customer count. Trust earned on the PDP must travel to the close.

Target: +10–15% checkout CVR
05

Mobile-First Redesign

The majority of Hike's traffic arrives via Meta Ads on mobile, making the mobile PDP the single most important commercial page in the business. Yet the current experience was optimized for desktop richness, not mobile conversion. Nine product images (including GIFs) created scroll debt before the customer even reached the CTA. Three critical trust badges (podiatrist-approved, free shipping, 30-day returns) were below the fold. Recommended fix: compress the gallery to 4 images above fold, surface all three trust signals within the first viewport, add a sticky Add-to-Cart bar triggered after price scroll.

Target: +12–18% mobile CVR
04 · Quick Wins

Ship today. No test required.

✦
Move "As Seen On" to the PDP. CBS, ABC, MSN, Yahoo: these logos exist on the homepage where no purchase decision is made. Move them to the product page where trust is needed most.
✦
Surface pain-specific reviews first. "Plantar fasciitis," "knee pain," "back pain": algorithmically prioritize reviews containing these keywords above generic five-star praise. They convert at a measurably higher rate for this audience.
✦
Free shipping in the first viewport on mobile. The most important reassurance for a first-time DTC buyer is currently below the fold on the primary device. Single CSS fix, immediate impact.
✦
Rewrite the return policy in customer language. "Not in love? Return within 30 days, no questions asked" outperforms legal-first copy on every trust metric. Always.
✦
Add attribution to the podiatrist claim. "Clinically reviewed by Dr. [Name], DPM" is dramatically more credible than "developed with podiatrists." Specificity is the mechanism of trust. Get the name on the page.
✦
Activate HSA/FSA eligible badge on PDP. This badge already appears on the homepage hero image; it's a powerful conversion signal for a health-condition buyer. It should be on every product page, near the price.

07 · Key Takeaways

What this engagement taught.

The product was not the problem

Strong health claim, real social proof, credible media coverage. The demand existed. The conversion gap was purely experiential: a sequencing and trust-continuity failure, not a product or audience problem.

Pain-point specificity converts: "comfort" doesn't

Generic benefit language massively underperforms condition-specific copy for a health-motivated buyer. Every touchpoint (ad, PDP, checkout) should speak to the exact condition, not the product category.

Trust must be continuous, not just built on the PDP

Building trust on the product page and losing it at checkout is the most common and most fixable conversion leak in DTC health brands. Every credibility signal must travel with the customer to the close.

Mobile is the funnel: build for it first

When paid social is the primary acquisition channel, the mobile PDP is the most important page in the business. Desktop optimization is secondary. Everything that earns the conversion happens on a 390px screen.

Bundles need a story, not just a discount percentage

Percentage-based discounts erode perceived value and train price-sensitive behavior. Use-case bundling ("one for work, one for weekends") increases relevance without touching unit margin.
